# Rationale

Purpose: keep the rules in SKILL.md from eroding. Each rule lists its basis, and derived rules are marked as derived. Read this before relaxing or "improving" a rule.

## Sources

1. **W3C COGA, "Making Content Usable for People with Cognitive and Learning
   Disabilities"** (W3C Working Group Note). Objectives used: clear and literal language, no reliance on memory, predictable design. https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/
2. **GOV.UK style guide.** Key word early in each sentence.
3. **Cognitive Load
   Theory** (Sweller). Remove extraneous load; missing connectives and unresolved sentences are extraneous load.
4. **Research on language-model
   calibration** (general). Stated confidence from chat-tuned models tends to be higher than their accuracy. Recorded from general knowledge, not from a specific paper checked for this file. Treat it as the motivating observation, not as a citation.
5. **Perception
   probes** (derived). A small informal probe: a few multiple-choice answers about one invented claim, asking how sure a sentence sounded, what the sentence said was checked, and what the next action would be. Treat the results as design input, not as a general finding.

## Rule → basis

| Rule                                                                      | Basis                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
|---------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Qualify evidence (source, scope, gap), no confidence scores               | **Derived.** A ranked confidence label describes the writer's feeling, which a model cannot measure (source 4). Source, scope, and gap describe what was shown, which is visible in the session. In the probe, sentences stating source and scope were read as intended in every case tried. A ranked label also hid a real gap in testing: a result from a smaller test input was presented at the same level as a result for the full input. |
| Fixed source markers                                                      | COGA predictability objective. Stable phrases are recognized without re-reading; new wording for a known source reads as a new kind of claim.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| Unlabeled claim = nothing checked                                         | **Derived, from probes.** With the convention known, an unlabeled claim was read as unchecked; the same claim, read cold, sounded "fairly sure". Both readings make an unlabeled checked claim misleading, so every claim carries a marker.                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| Docs marker adds "I have not run it"                                      | **Derived, from probes.** "The docs confirm" was read as strongly as an observed run. Documentation states intended behavior, not observed behavior, so the marker names the gap.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| "From memory" and "my guess" both say "not checked"                       | **Derived, from probes.** Recall and inference were read as equally unsure by engineering standards. Both are unchecked; the marker says so, and the difference (recall vs reasoning from Y) is stated as scope.                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| No hedging "should"                                                       | **Derived, from probes.** "Should" was read as unsure without a stated reason, and it is ambiguous with the requirement sense.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Options in "I can't decide this for you" carry their own sources          | **Derived, from probes.** A choice between unsourced facts prompted the question of where the facts came from, before any choice.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| The writer states the gap; the reader judges whether it matters           | **Derived, from probes.** The same gap ("not tested under load") was irrelevant or relevant depending on a goal the writer could not know.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| First sentence: no bare Yes/No when qualified; carries the evidence scope | **Derived.** A bare verdict followed by qualifying text makes the first sentence false as read. The reader who stops after the first sentence must not come away with a wider claim than the evidence supports.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| "Not checked" line when reporting results                                 | **Derived.** Unchecked parts discovered later, by asking, cost more trust than the same parts stated up front.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Warm words optional, only after evidence                                  | **Derived, from probes.** "I'm confident" alone prompted an independent test of the claim: it carried no weight. With evidence before it, it raised perceived certainty above the evidence alone. So it is allowed only when the evidence covers the reader's goal, where raising perceived certainty is justified.                                                                                                                            |
| Passing tests described by whether they could fail                        | **Derived.** Tests generated against current behavior, tests with trivial assertions, and tests that mock the changed path pass whether or not the code is correct. Stating which kind of test it was shows the gap.                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| Warmth is literal, no tone-only meaning                                   | COGA literal-language objective. Irony, understatement, emoji, and exclamation marks carry meaning that some readers do not decode, including many autistic readers.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| No filler phrases such as "Good news"                                     | **Derived, from probes.** On a pass-or-fail result, the phrase added nothing the result did not already say.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Warmth targets evidence, not the reader                                   | **Derived.** Praise of the reader carries no information about the claim and reads as flattery.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| No word caps                                                              | **Derived.** Hard caps remove linking words and evidence clauses first, because those look optional. The result reads fast but is re-read many times, so total reading effort rises.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| Full sentences with linking words                                         | Cognitive Load Theory: without "because", "so", or "but", the reader must infer the relation between sentences.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| No anthropomorphism of things                                             | COGA literal-language objective. Also hides the real agent. The writer's own "I ran" or "I have not checked" is excluded, because it reports the writer's actions.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| Key word early                                                            | GOV.UK style guide, plus working-memory limits.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| No stranded prepositions in restrictive relative clauses                  | **Derived.** The pronoun's role stays unresolved until the clause ends, and several common first languages forbid this pattern, so non-native readers get no help from their own grammar.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Caveat next to claim                                                      | COGA: do not make the reader carry information between sections.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| Per-row sources in tables                                                 | Same basis as caveat next to claim: a note below a table forces the reader to map sources back to rows.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Refer to items by name, not by number                                     | **Derived.** Numbers the reader cannot see, or that change with ordering, point to the wrong item.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| No invented terms                                                         | COGA clear-language objective: the reader must be able to look a term up.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Answer first, labeled optional sections                                   | COGA: make the purpose clear immediately and let readers skip what they do not need.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |

## Design decisions

- **Qualify, not
  quantify.** The first draft used five ranked confidence levels with fixed openers. It was replaced after probes and review showed that the ranking itself was a quantity, that its wording was read differently from its intent at three of five levels, and that it hid scope gaps. Do not reintroduce ranked levels.
- **Standalone skill with an on/off switch.** It names no other skill, so it works when installed alone.
- **Conflicts: the more inclusive instruction
  wins.** An instruction written to accommodate readers with disabilities, including neurodivergent readers, wins over one written for speed, brevity, or style. Between two accessibility rules, the one that leaves less to infer or remember wins. A named, testable criterion replaces the earlier "stricter rule wins", which could not be decided for rules that constrain different things.
- **Domain: technical prose written while doing a
  task.** The goal is faster completion of local or internal work through first-read comprehension. Excluded: casual, personal, and creative writing; general science explanations outside a task; formal deliverables for audit or oversight bodies, which follow that body's required format.
- **Part
  scope:** Part 1 and Part 2 apply to chat replies and working documents; Part 3 applies to chat only, because documents are read later and out of context.
- **Design premise: the model stays a black
  box.** A confidence figure produced by the model does not survive into, or reliably describe, the reasoning behind a statement, and this is expected to remain true for years. Confidence is therefore assessed only from traceable facts about how the statement was produced: what was run, what was read, what was recalled, what was inferred.
- **Test evidence table in a reference
  file.** The weak-test cases load only when a reply cites checks as evidence, which keeps the always-loaded body smaller.

## Known tensions

- **Warmth vs literal
  readers.** Some readers find any emotional wording noisy. Warm words are therefore optional and tied to evidence. The off switch exists for readers who prefer none.
- **Longer
  replies.** Source, scope, and gap make replies longer. The answer-first shape and skippable sections keep the cost of that length optional.
- **Marker
  fatigue.** Labeling every actionable claim can crowd short replies. Resolution: label claims the reader might act on; background explanation that the reader will not act on needs no marker.
- **Probe
  limits.** The probe was small and used one low-risk claim. Readers may treat the same evidence differently for a risky change. Re-probe before changing the markers.
